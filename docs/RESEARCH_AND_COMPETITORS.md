# Research and Competitor Review

## Market context
The event-planning market includes broad consumer tools, spreadsheet-based planning methods, and niche vendor management products. Many offerings are either too generic or too fragmented across separate tools.

This is an initial positioning hypothesis, not validated competitor research. No competitor interviews, pricing comparison, or source-backed feature matrix has been completed.

## Competitive themes
### Spreadsheet-first tools
- Strength: flexible, familiar for planning and budgets.
- Weakness: weak permissions, poor UX, and limited event-specific workflows.

### Wedding planning apps
- Strength: tailored to milestone events and guest experience planning.
- Weakness: less useful for multi-event commercial and professional organizers.

### Project-management tools adapted for events
- Strength: task and timeline management is solid.
- Weakness: budget tracking, vendor management, and RSVP workflows are not purpose-built.

## Intended positioning for EventPilot AI
EventPilot AI aims to sit between generic project planning software and wedding-specific tools. The current MVP has separate sections for budget, vendors, guest records, and deterministic planning notes; it does not yet provide a complete event lifecycle or AI-assisted strategy.

## Differentiators
- event-centric data model rather than generic tasks
- secure ownership at the event level
- deterministic business logic for budget and planning reviews
- AI suggestions that remain reviewable and non-destructive
- strong focus on planner workflow, not just social sharing

## Risks and watch-outs
- AI features must not silently mutate sensitive event data.
- Ownership logic must be enforced server-side.
- Users expect quick value; the first event creation flow must be remarkably simple.
